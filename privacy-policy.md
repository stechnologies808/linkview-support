# Linkview Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 30, 2026

Linkview shows information about your device's network connection. It has no accounts, ads or analytics, and the developer does not receive or store your data.

## Information read on your device

Linkview reads your connection type, Wi-Fi network name, local IP addresses, gateway, DNS servers and VPN status so it can display them. This information stays on your device.

## Location permission

iOS requires precise location permission before an app can read the name of your Wi-Fi network. Linkview uses this permission only to display the network name. It does not read, store or share your location.

## Outage history and widget

Linkview saves a history of connection problems, including when they happened and the network name, on your device. The widget shows your connection status, network name, public IP address and provider. None of this is sent to the developer. Deleting the app deletes this data.

## Local network

If your internet stops working, Linkview may ask for Local Network access so it can check whether your router is responding. It only contacts your router, and only to see if it answers.

## Third-party services

Like any web service, each of these receives your IP address when Linkview contacts it. Each one's handling of that data is governed by its own privacy policy.

- **ipify (api64.ipify.org):** shows your public IP address and measures latency.
- **rdap.org and the regional internet registries it forwards to:** Linkview sends your public IP address to look up which provider it belongs to.
- **Apple (www.apple.com):** the app and widget load a small test page to check that the internet is working.
- **Cloudflare (1.1.1.1), Google (8.8.8.8) and Quad9 (9.9.9.9):** Linkview briefly connects to these servers to check that the internet is reachable. It doesn't send them any other data.

These checks run about every 30 seconds while the app is on screen, and periodically for the widget.
